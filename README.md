# Global Disease Burden Analysis: Mental Disorders Share of DALYs (1990-2019) with Epidemiological Forecasting for Vietnam

## 🩺 Background & Medical Context
As a medical student focusing on preventive medicine and public health analytics, understanding the macroeconomic trajectory of disease burden is critical. Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) serve as a core metric to quantify the collective burden of diseases. 

While clinical studies often focus on individual psychiatric cohorts, this longitudinal epidemiological project focuses on evaluating how mental and substance use disorders have evolved as a share of the total disease burden over the past three decades.

## 🎯 Analytical Objectives
- To conduct a comparative epidemiological analysis benchmarking the mental health disease burden (% of total DALYs) in Vietnam against regional and global baselines (Singapore, Thailand, and Global averages) from 1990 to 2019.
- To implement a machine learning-based Linear Regression model to forecast the future public health burden of mental disorders in Vietnam for the milestone years 2025 and 2030.

## 📊 Methodology & Analytical Results
- **Trend Visualizations:** Built using Python (Pandas, Seaborn, and Matplotlib) to map out historical health burden shifts. Vietnam demonstrated a steady and continuous rise, climbing from a **3.29%** share of total disease DALYs in 1990 to **4.63%** in 2019.
- **Predictive Analytics:** Utilizing a linear regression training pipeline, the model projects that mental disorders will continue to demand a higher share of national health resources, forecasting a gánh nặng up to **5.08% in 2025** and reaching **5.28% by 2030**.

## 🛡️ Public Health and Preventive Policy Implications
1. **Strategic Healthcare Resource Allocation:** The projected linear increase of nearly ~14% in overall mental health burden share by 2030 underscores an urgent need to expand psychiatric and psychological infrastructure in primary care.
2. **Academic & Youth Mental Health Frameworks:** These macroeconomic trends provide a solid empirical baseline supporting the necessity of institutional mental health interventions, particularly targeting high-stress cohorts such as university student populations.
